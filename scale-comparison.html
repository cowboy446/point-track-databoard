What changes

Clip scale

One ratio across every source, target, point and frame in the clip. This reproduces the published evaluation.

Target-view/time scale

αv,t=mean⁡q∈(v,t)∣GTz,q∣mean⁡q∈(v,t)∣predz,q∣\alpha_{v,t}=\frac{\operatorname{mean}_{q\in(v,t)}|\mathrm{GT}_{z,q}|}{\operatorname{mean}_{q\in(v,t)}|\mathrm{pred}_{z,q}|}αv,t​=meanq∈(v,t)​∣predz,q​∣meanq∈(v,t)​∣GTz,q​∣​

48 ratios per clip. Each group pools the queries from all three sources; sources are not aligned independently. Samples, visibility and point IDs stay fixed.

XYZ and diagnostic loss

E=mean⁡∥world⁡(α pred)−GTworld∥2E=\operatorname{mean}\left\|\operatorname{world}(\alpha\,\mathrm{pred})-\mathrm{GT}_{\mathrm{world}}\right\|_2E=mean∥world(αpred)−GTworld​∥2​

Alignment is applied in the target camera before the existing camera-to-world transform. The local loss normalizes prediction and GT separately within each target-view/time group. Confidence and visibility terms stay fixed.

Interpretation

Local alignment uses GT to correct view-specific and time-varying scale errors. It may improve spatial overlap while changing motion. The CSV includes mean-centered trajectory error in meters for both rules. Four clips cannot establish a general benchmark result.
