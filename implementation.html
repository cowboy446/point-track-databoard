Shared design

Production focuses on rigid bodies and articulated rigid mechanisms, including robot links, drawers and doors. SIM1 cloth simulation is a small exploratory study and has not entered production.

The common output is a calibrated observation sequence: RGB-D, persistent surface points, robot state and actions. The way that sequence is produced depends on what the source actually records.

  1. Resolve the sourceTask, assets, robot, trajectory and code version
  2. Establish stateRestore recorded state or execute a physical rollout
  3. Capture togetherRGB, depth, geometry IDs and camera transforms
  4. Track surfacesFixed local bindings, validity and visibility
  5. Validate & publishH5, manifest and source evidence
  • Preserve the physical trajectory when changing appearance. A render seed may change approved lights, textures or cameras. A new object layout requires a new physical plan. Plan counts and render counts are recorded separately.
  • Give every frame a defined clock. Record the observation time, camera pose and action convention. Restoring state and advancing physics are different operations; the table below identifies which one each adapter uses.
  • Bind points to surfaces. Rigid points follow their owning geometry; deformable points follow fixed mesh triangles and barycentric coordinates. IDs persist within an episode. Validity and per-camera visibility are separate masks.
  • Retain native action semantics. Demonstration actions, drive targets and observed joints are not interchangeable. Each adapter records its own alignment and units.
  • Separate task outcome from data integrity. Successful-plan collection filters task failures; VLA and cloth trials can retain complete unsuccessful rollouts with explicit outcome labels.
  • Publish from durable evidence. Freeze source identifiers and settings, validate completed files, and use ledgers or stage records for retries. The H5 encoding is shared; simulation workers and scheduling remain adapter-specific.

The catalogue contains 57,459 episodes from InternData, LIBERO, robotwin-random and RoboCasa365. Lift2, GenieSim and SIM1 are documented here as separate campaigns. Implementation evidence was read from the gateway and nodes 42–45 on 20 October 2026.

Rendering pitfalls

Observed problemWhat we changed
Near surfaces disappear or reveal object interiorsInternData wrist cameras clipped nearby surfaces at 5 cm. Reducing the near plane to 1 cm resolved the recorded close-range blackouts. Check clipping before diagnosing physical penetration; choose the range for the working distance and verify depth precision.
Images lag behind camera posesInternData reset could leave stale first-frame buffers and mismatched views. Update all camera mounts before rendering, then read the buffers. Warm up the first frame at the recorded state.
Replay objects or fingers drift during synchronizationThe FK synchronization tick can move objects and passive joints through gravity, contact or drives. Reapply recorded robot and object states after synchronization, before capture.

Replay and time

The recorder saves joint values, robot roots, object roots, articulated-object joints and visibility. Replay restores those values, uses a physics tick to synchronize forward kinematics, then reapplies the authoritative states before rendering. Gravity, contacts and drives are suppressed for replay synchronization. This stage reproduces a saved sequence; it does not test grasp success again. Output timestamps are a uniform retiming of recorded samples, not a recovered map to every original physics step.

Augmentation and scheduling

Both catalogue shards target 20 successful plans per task × 5 renders per plan. Failed attempts and partially completed groups make the published count differ from that target: node42 contributes 9,510 episodes and node43 contributes 19,949. A plan seed controls the original scene; each render keeps its motion and changes the approved appearance and agent-camera mounts.

A SHA256-derived seed per prim controls color multipliers in [0.55, 1.0], roughness multipliers in [0.65, 1.35] clipped to [0.05, 1.0], and light intensity scaled by 2e for e ∈ [−0.6, 0.6]. Engine hooks also select environment maps and fixture textures. Each agent camera receives one translation of at most 5 cm and one rotation of at most 5° per episode; robot-camera mounts are unchanged. Resolved appearance layers, hashes and actual camera transforms are saved.

Deterministic appearance factors S3
def appearance_factors(seed, path):
    token = int(hashlib.sha256(f"{seed}/{path}".encode()).hexdigest()[:16], 16)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(token)
    return rng.uniform(0.55, 1.0, 3), float(rng.uniform(0.65, 1.35)), float(rng.uniform(-0.6, 0.6))

Source lineage and modifications

The comparison base is upstream commit 2a0a21f. Local commit e59a06d introduced replay stabilization; it is a local patch, not an upstream release. Four tracked runtime files differ in the deployed tree:

  • world_recorder: supplemental joint / root / articulated-object capture, explicit replay mode, state locking and restoration, render synchronization, and a second robot-state restore after the FK tick.
  • custom_camera: accept recorded reference poses and avoid updating a mount after its image has been rendered.
  • simbox_dual_workflow: capture supplemental state during planning, prime frame-zero camera buffers and synchronize all camera mounts before image reads.
  • planner_utils: support both old and new Drake parser and contact-configuration APIs.

QuARC adds separate adapters for multi-view capture, point bindings, staged processing, quotas and H5 export. The material repair is in this layer: resolved USD texture paths are anchored before copying attributes between layers; built-in MDL lookup is retained; unresolved assets stop the copy. Restoring properties individually avoids replacing a whole session layer and invalidating live articulation handles. Missing floor / wall fixtures are added as metric geometry for RGB-D. The Lift2 ceiling light is a separate campaign change.

Material repair · preserve asset resolution S4
def anchored_asset_value(value):
    from pxr import Sdf
    if isinstance(value, Sdf.AssetPath):
        if not value.path:
            return value
        if value.resolvedPath:
            return Sdf.AssetPath(value.resolvedPath)
        # MDL builtins are resolved by the renderer's module search path.
        if value.path.endswith('.mdl') and '/' not in value.path:
            return value
        if value.path.startswith(('http:', 'https:', 'omniverse:')):
            return value
        if Path(value.path).is_absolute() and Path(value.path).is_file():
            return value
        raise ValueError('Unresolved USD asset before cross-layer copy: '+value.path)
    if isinstance(value, Sdf.AssetPathArray):
        return Sdf.AssetPathArray([anchored_asset_value(v) for v in value])
    return value

Lift2 · fixture-qualified campaign

Lift2 uses the same state-replay approach, with its own frozen release and lighting. This production campaign selects 205 pick-and-place configurations: 80 assigned to node42 and 125 to node44. The target is 50 plans × 1 render per configuration. Conveyor, articulated-object, fluid and long-horizon tasks are outside this campaign.

The fixture repair restores table / conveyor obstacles to planning where applicable and adds native PhysX contact evidence. Every plan must pass the fixture-contact gate at a 3 mm penetration limit. The first H5 for each configuration also undergoes an offline robot–fixture geometry check, joint alignment and source-plan hash verification before the remaining quota is released in windows of at most 20 candidates. The offline first-sample check and the per-plan native check are separate gates.

The ceiling uses a 3 × 3 m rectangular light at z = 2.3 m, intensity 2,000 and normalize=false, configured only for Lift2. Agent-camera baselines are lowered to match this robot. Each node runs two workers per GPU across eight GPUs, with four CPU postprocess workers and a 128 GiB spool budget. Frozen profiles enable accuracy checks. Current watchdog limits are 600 s for initialization, 1,200 s for planning, 7,200 s for replay/render and 3,600 s without progress.

RoboTwin ↗ / SAPIEN is the simulator behind robotwin-random, the appearance-randomized variant used in this production batch. It supports five dual-arm embodiments. The batch reuses 50 demonstrations for each of 230 task–embodiment pairs: 11,500 H5 files. Head, two wrists and an observer camera produce 640 × 480 images at 25 fps.

The source contains joint paths, not a complete per-frame object-state tape. The adapter reconstructs the initial task and drives the recorded arm trajectory through physics.
